# Building Redstone

<a href="https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/redstone/id6755842069">Redstone</a> is my SpaceX launch tracker. I put version 1.0 on the App Store on 29 November 2025. Version 2.0 followed on 31 October 2026. The release notes call it a new app, rebuilt from scratch.

The Apple apps talk to a Rust server.

## Missions

Missions is the first tab. The next launch is a card at the top. The countdown runs to the opening of the window. Under it, a bar draws the window in GMT and marks sunset. A launch window lasts, so the card shows the span. Following, Upcoming and Past come under the card.

On iPhone, Missions, Stats, Starship and Search sit in a bottom bar. On iPad those same tabs move to the top, and the missions list stays a narrow column. The App Store marks Redstone as designed for iPad.

## Flight details

A flight repeats the countdown, then a facts table, then an overview. The rows are the vehicle, the booster and which flight that booster is on, the launch site, the landing, and the mission type. From the flight screen I can set a reminder, add the launch to the calendar, or share it. Watch live is on that screen.

## Stats and Starbase

Stats is the launch record. It totals missions, landings and reflights, shows launches by year, splits landings by site, and counts each vehicle. Starship has its own tab. The tab lists Starbase road closures, a place and a time range for each one, then every Starship flight with its ship, its booster and the site.

Widgets put the next launch on the Home Screen and the Lock Screen. A Live Activity counts down on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. A reminder tells me when a launch is on today. The listing includes iPhone on iOS 26, iPad on iPadOS 26, and Apple Watch on watchOS 26. It can also run on an Apple silicon Mac from macOS 26, and the store says it is not verified for macOS.

Redstone is free. The privacy listing says the app collects no data. It is independent of SpaceX.
